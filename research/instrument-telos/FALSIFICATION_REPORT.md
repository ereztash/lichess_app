# Falsification report

An honest attempt to kill H1, and then the opposite test: is the product already aligning effort
with value, with only isolated exceptions?

## Counterexamples found against H1

**F-1. The core loop pays every single time, and it is most of the burden.**
Decomposed from the two walks: about 83 percent of real taps in a live game are board, submit and
continuation, and every one of those decisions returns a full reveal. The cost the player pays most
often is the cost that repays fastest. A product optimised around the instrument would not have its
cheapest, most frequent action be its most reliably repaid one.

**F-2. The reads pay on the next screen, and the repository already ran the delete test on them.**
`nextQuestion` consumes `statedUnknown` to anchor the reveal's question to the player's own
sentence, and `shared/reveal.ts` calls that text *"the one thing on screen the engine did not
produce."* More damaging to H1: these fields were once required on every decision and were cut to a
sample with the reason written down, *"on six decisions out of seven two of those three bought
nothing measurable at all"*, and *"an instrument too expensive to use is not a more careful
instrument."* **The repository has already performed this mission's central test, on this exact
field, and acted against its own data appetite.** The Calibration Loop found this independently and
called it the recorded refusal the lens was said to lack.

**F-3. The burden reallocation was argued, measured and written down.**
`ASK_RATE` was lowered to pay for `PROBE_PROBABILITY` with the arithmetic in the file: 18 extra
questions per forty-move game became 20, and the share of decisions carrying an extra step went
from 40 to 45 percent. A product that had never asked who the burden is for does not produce that
paragraph.

**F-4. The instrument layer is about a fifth of the taps.**
Measured: 5 of 29 real taps in walk A, 4 of 20 in walk B. The Calibration Loop's warning applies
here and cuts the same way: tap cost is not beneficiary, but a mechanism that is supposed to make
the player an operator of the instrument should not be confined to a fifth of their hands.

**F-5. The best value contract in the product is at the point of highest cost.**
The import panel states, in one sentence, what the retrospective corpus structurally cannot hold
and that no quantity fixes it. That is the exact shape H1 says the product fails to provide, and
the product provides it, unprompted, at the moment it asks for prospective effort.

**F-6. The product refuses the claims that would make the tax profitable.**
No rating, no score, no streak, no badge, *"לא ימליץ מה ללמוד, הוא מודד, לא מאמן"*, and a goal note
that says no number here measures distance to the player's own goal. An instrument-first product
harvests attention with exactly those devices. This one has removed all of them.

## Counterexamples found against the alternatives

**Against H7, no structural problem.** Two uncovered costs share a structure rather than a mood: the
counterfactual's immediate payoff sits behind a disclosure, and the blitz game's payoff sits behind
finishing. In both, **the payoff exists and is not where the cost is paid.** That is one mechanism
appearing twice on independent surfaces, which is what `DR_PLAN_0.md` said would raise a structural
reading. It is a smaller and more specific mechanism than H1.

**Against H2, legibility alone.** The two denominators cannot be fixed by wording, because the
question they raise is which lane the player is in, and `D26` records that as an owner decision
rather than a sentence.

**Against H3, time-to-value alone.** The per-decision payoff is immediate. H3 describes the
threshold region correctly and is silent about everything else.

**Against H4, the missing learning layer.** The longest unpaid stretch, Path C, lies entirely inside
the reachable half and would not be shortened by turning the flag on. H4 explains the terminus and
not the interval.

## The opposite test: is the product already well aligned?

Largely, and the ledger says so before any interpretation: nine of sixteen reachable rows are
`VALUE_ALIGNED` or `NATURAL_CAPTURE`, two are deliberately valueless to the instrument, three are
covered tax, **two are uncovered**, and one needs FIELD. Five differentiated payoffs are available
immediately. The exceptions are isolated and nameable.

## What this does to the hypothesis

H1, as stated, is **refuted where it is most testable and survives only where it is least
distinguishable.** The Calibration Loop reached the same partition independently:

* per-decision region: **refuted**;
* record-page region: **category error**, the cost is zero and the defect is trust and ontology;
* accumulation and flag regions: **survives, and is indistinguishable from H3 and H4 on repository
  evidence.**

The corrections this report makes to the audit's own earlier framing:

1. The ledger's `UNCOVERED_EPISTEMIC_TAX` on the counterfactual's immediate step is better named
   **a salience defect**. The payoff exists and is two taps away.
2. The two denominators do not belong in an instrument-burden analysis at all. Rendering them costs
   nothing.
3. The "26 taps bought 1 of 60" framing over-states the case. It is the **designed** rate, `P(>= 1)`
   of 0.386, and the real number is that the first claim costs about 401 decisions. The defect is
   that the product never says so.

## What would still overturn this report

A FIELD participant who passes M1 to M5 at `A0`, fails M6, and when asked what continuing buys
answers in terms of the system rather than themselves while pointing at the counter. That is the
operator posture, no competing hypothesis here predicts it, and nothing in the repository can
produce or exclude it.
