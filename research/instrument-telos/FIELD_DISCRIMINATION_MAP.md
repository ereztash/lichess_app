question is only what its existing M1 to M8 already separate.

## Where each hypothesis lives

* **H1** lives in **M6 and M7**, and only there. It predicts that a participant who understands the
  product and can operate it will decline the next unit of effort because they cannot see what it
  buys them.
* **H2** lives in M1, M2, M3 and M8. It predicts failures of understanding at surfaces whose payoff
  is `AVAILABLE_NOW`.
* **H3** lives in M6 and M7 alongside H1, and is separated from it by **what the participant says
  when asked what continuing buys.** H3's participant names the right payoff and judges it too far.
  H1's participant cannot name a payoff at all.
* **H4** is barely observable in FIELD, because the flagged states are unreachable. It is decidable
  from the repository, and `TELOS_CHAIN.md` decides it.
* **H5** lives in the non-pointing denominator probe inside M2 and M5.
* **Founder translation** is observable as **where the first `A2` or `A3` was needed**, which the
  participant file records as a required field.
* **H6** lives in M7 conditional on M1 to M6 passing, which the frozen rules already isolate.

## The mandatory question: what FIELD outcome makes H1 weaker?

Four, in decreasing order of strength.

**D-1. M6 passes at `A0` and the participants name a payoff the product can actually produce.**
If asked *"מה אתה מצפה לקבל אם תמשיך?"* they answer with the reveal, the next question, or the
inference limits, and judge that worth the next decision, then the costs are being read as covered
by the people paying them. H1 is a description of the architecture that the users do not
experience. **This is the single strongest falsifier and it is directly measured by M6.**

**D-2. M7 passes while M5 fails.** Participants continue without prompting and cannot separate "the
system knows more" from "I improved". That is pull without comprehension, which H1 cannot explain:
an instrument-operator does not continue, and a confused-but-engaged player is H2 and H5 territory.

**D-3. The first assistance needed is `A2` at the front door, before any evidence exists.**
Nothing has been extracted from the player at that point, so a failure there is a value-contract or
legibility failure. H1 requires the failure to appear **after** costs have been paid.

**D-4. M1 to M5 pass and M7 fails, with participants who say plainly that they do not want the
thing the product offers.** The frozen rules already call that a value-and-pull problem and forbid
repairing it as UX. It is also not H1: a player who understands the contract, judges it fair, and
does not want the product has no instrument–telos gap, they have a market mismatch.

**What would strengthen H1:** participants who pass M1 to M5 at `A0`, fail M6, and when asked what
continuing buys, answer in terms of the system rather than themselves, for example "it needs more
data" or "so it can tell me something eventually", **and** name the counter as the thing that
moves. That is the operator posture the hypothesis predicts, and no other hypothesis here predicts
it.

## Why this map is not a rescue device

H1 is credited by exactly one pattern, M6 failing with system-shaped answers after M1 to M5 pass at
`A0`. It is weakened by four named patterns, each of which is reachable inside the existing frozen
protocol without changing a single measure. A hypothesis that can absorb every outcome is a lens,
and this one has been given four ways to lose before any participant was recruited.
