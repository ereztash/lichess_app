## The two constraints this execution establishes

**1. The protocol cannot be designed before the rule class is.** A think-aloud protocol is a coding
scheme over episodes, and an episode is defined by an item. Items come from a rule class. **There is
no valid rule class**, so there is nothing to code.

**2. Two coders and a κ are necessary and nowhere near sufficient.** High inter-coder agreement on
*"this episode shows trigger recognition"* would establish that two people read a transcript the
same way. It would establish nothing about whether the coded category corresponds to a state that
takes a different intervention. **Low agreement is evidence against the coding construct; high
agreement is not evidence for it.**

## What is fixed now, so it cannot be chosen after seeing results

Declared here because declaring it later is the failure this programme exists to prevent.

- **Reliability statistics chosen in advance by design, not by result:** Cohen's κ for two coders on
  a nominal scheme; Fleiss' κ for three or more; Krippendorff's α where coders differ per episode or
  the scheme is ordinal. **Reported for every category separately**, never pooled into one number —
  a scheme can be reliable on `CALCULATION` and at chance on `DIAGNOSIS`, and the pooled figure hides
  exactly the category the programme needs.
- **Category changes after seeing agreement are recorded, with both the before and after values.**
  Redefining a category to raise κ is legitimate and is also a result.
- **`AMBIGUOUS` and `MULTIPLE-STRATEGIES` are first-class codes**, not residuals. An episode forced
  into a unique mechanism is a coding artefact, and
  [`MULTI_STRATEGY_REGISTER.md`](MULTI_STRATEGY_REGISTER.md) lists ten mechanisms that produce the
  headline observation.
- **The comparison is against `move + time`, not against zero.** The question is incremental value
  ([`INCREMENTAL_EVIDENCE_VALUE.md`](INCREMENTAL_EVIDENCE_VALUE.md)), and the cheap baseline is
  already collected.
