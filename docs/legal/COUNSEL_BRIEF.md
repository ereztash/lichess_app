# Brief for counsel: four questions on the Decision Lab / Stockfish licensing boundary

**Prepared:** 2026-09-14, against `490aed07b0ec2e2d27ab7574eacdb7673a7d5666`
**Prepared by:** engineering. **This is not legal advice and states no legal conclusion.**

This brief asks four specific questions. It deliberately does not ask for a general review of the
business, the product or the codebase. Each question has a concrete answer that changes what may be
built next, and each is accompanied by the facts needed to answer it.

Attachments: `IP_PROVENANCE_AUDIT.md`, `LICENSING.md`, `docs/licensing/GPL_CUTOFF.md`,
`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.

## Background in one paragraph

Decision Lab is a chess decision-measurement instrument. It is a browser application that ships and
serves the Stockfish chess engine (GPL-3.0-or-later) as WebAssembly. The repository is public and
its root `LICENSE` currently places the whole project under GPL-3.0-or-later — adopted explicitly,
in the licence file's own words, as a conservative compliance posture rather than as a conclusion
that the integration creates one combined work. The owner wishes to develop future first-party code
under proprietary terms while keeping Stockfish GPL and compliant, without disturbing rights already
granted.

**Why it matters:** everything else depends on it. If the rights are not held, nothing can be
relicensed regardless of how the Stockfish boundary is characterised.

**Facts.** 444 commits. Authorship resolves to three parties:

| `Claude <noreply@anthropic.com>` | 256 | AI-authored via Claude Code, on Erez Tash's account |
| `dependabot[bot]` | 2 | mechanical dependency version bumps |

There is **no third-party human contributor** and no external contribution has ever been accepted.
No first-party file asserts foreign copyright: a scan for `Copyright (c)`,
`SPDX-License-Identifier`, `@license` and `Licensed under` across `client/src`, `server`, `shared`
and `scripts` returns zero non-Decision-Lab matches. No vendored third-party directory and no
committed minified bundle exists under any first-party path.

**What we specifically need answered:**

1a. Does Erez Tash hold sufficient rights in the first-party source to relicense it under
proprietary terms?

1b. **The AI-authorship point, which we want addressed directly rather than assumed away.** The
majority of the tree by line count was generated by an AI coding tool. Two sub-questions:

- *Ownership* — a contractual matter under the tool provider's terms. We have not interpreted the
  contract and would like it read.
- *Copyrightability* — whether material generated without sufficient human authorship attracts
  copyright at all in the relevant jurisdictions.

Our working understanding, which we are asking you to confirm or correct, is that **1b does not
block proprietary distribution**, because unpublished source cannot be copied from and distribution
can be governed by contract irrespective of whether copyright subsists. What it would affect is the
strength of the remedy against a copier of any *published* code. If that understanding is wrong, it
changes the plan materially.

1c. Are any steps needed now to consolidate or evidence the rights — an assignment, a record of
ownership, a change to how the tool is used?

## Question 2 — Does the current architecture permit proprietary distribution of the Decision Lab side?

**Why it matters:** this is the question the current whole-repository GPL licence was adopted to
avoid answering.

**The architecture, as mechanically verified.** Full evidence in `IP_PROVENANCE_AUDIT.md` §6.

- Decision Lab references the engine through Vite `?url` imports, which evaluate to a **string
  path**. No engine code enters the application module graph.
- The engine runs in a **Web Worker**: a separate global scope and thread, with no shared address
  space with the application.
- Communication is `postMessage` carrying **UCI protocol text** — the standard chess-engine
  protocol, implemented by many independent engines and many independent front-ends.
- Every emitted application chunk was scanned for engine linkage markers (`_ZN8Stockfish`,
  `asmLibraryArg`, `wasmMemory`, `NNUE evaluation`): **zero matches**. The same detector found
  **49** in the `.wasm`, so the zero is a measurement and not a broken test.
- The engine and the application are **separate files** in the build output, loaded separately.
- The engine is shipped **unmodified**; distributed SHA-256 hashes are identical to the published
  package's.
- The server does not execute Stockfish. Engine execution is client-side only.

**What we specifically need answered:**

2a. Under GPL-3.0, does this arrangement constitute two separate programs communicating, or a
single combined work?

2b. If separate: does shipping both in one distribution constitute **mere aggregation** under
GPL-3.0's aggregation clause, such that Decision Lab may be distributed under proprietary terms?

2c. Are there facts about a **Web Worker** specifically — as distinct from a separate OS process
communicating over a pipe or socket — that change the analysis? We regard this as the crux. A
Worker has a separate global scope and communicates only by structured-clone message passing, but
it is not a separate OS process in every browser, and we do not want to rely on an analogy we have
not had tested.

2d. If the answer is that they are a combined work, what is the **minimum** architectural change
that would make them separate? For example: serving the engine from a distinct origin, shipping it
as a user-supplied component, or moving it out of the distribution entirely.

## Question 3 — Does the current distribution satisfy Stockfish's GPL obligations?

**Why it matters:** this obligation exists today, under the current licence, and will continue to
exist under any outcome of Question 2. It is the one question whose answer cannot be deferred.

**Facts.**

- Conveyed: `stockfish-18-lite-single.wasm` (7,295,411 bytes) and its loader, served from the
  deployment.
- Licence text conveyed with the build and served at `/licenses/stockfish/COPYING.txt`.
- `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` names the component, copyright holder, version and licence.
- Corresponding source offered as public upstream repositories at the matching version:
  `github.com/nmrugg/stockfish.js` (the WASM build) and
  `github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish` (the engine), at no charge.
- **Unmodified**, verified by hash. GPL §5 is therefore not engaged.
- One recorded discrepancy: npm metadata declares `GPL-3.0`; upstream releases
  `GPL-3.0-or-later`; our notices state `GPL-3.0-or-later`.

**What we specifically need answered:**

3a. Do the notice and licence-conveyance arrangements satisfy GPL-3.0 §4?

3b. Does offering the corresponding source as a **public upstream repository URL at a matching
version** satisfy §6 for object code conveyed over a network, or is a pinned upstream commit —
with the build relationship to the exact artifact hashes — required? We are prepared to do the
stricter thing and would like to know whether we must.

3c. Does serving the engine from our own deployment, rather than linking to a third party's copy,
change what we owe?

3d. Is anything required that we are not currently doing?

## Question 4 — Is the bridge an independent work or a derivative of Stockfish?

**Why it matters:** this is the narrowest question and the one with the cleanest remedy. If the
bridge is derivative, one file moves to the GPL side. If it is independent, the boundary holds as
designed. Either answer is actionable; not knowing is what is expensive.

**The file.** `client/src/lib/stockfish.ts`, 369 lines, first-party. It:

- imports the engine as a **URL string**, not as a module;
- constructs a `Worker` from that URL;
- writes UCI command strings and parses UCI response lines;
- compiles to a 3,974-byte chunk containing **zero** engine linkage markers;
- contains **no** Stockfish algorithm, no transcribed Stockfish source, and no Stockfish header.

UCI is a published, engine-independent protocol. The bridge was written against the protocol.

**One fact we are volunteering because it cuts the other way.** `scripts/sf-wasm.mjs` — a research
harness wrapper, **not distributed**, absent from the build output — calls `require("stockfish")`
and drives the engine **in-process**. We have placed this file on the GPL side of our boundary and
allowlisted it there. We raise it because it is the single place in the repository where
first-party code and engine code share a process, and we would rather you assess it than discover
it.

**What we specifically need answered:**

4a. Is `client/src/lib/stockfish.ts` an independent work that communicates with Stockfish, or a
work derived from it?

4b. Does writing to a published engine protocol, without reference to engine source, keep an
adapter independent?

4c. Is our treatment of `scripts/sf-wasm.mjs` — first-party code held on the GPL side because it
loads the engine in-process — correct, over-cautious, or insufficient?

4d. Would parsing richer engine output (principal variations, evaluation internals, NNUE-specific
fields) change the analysis, as against parsing bare best-move and score?

## What we will not do until these are answered

- We will **not** represent the proprietary external distribution model as legally validated.
- We will **not** remove or weaken any Stockfish GPL compliance material.
- We will **not** assert that historical GPL grants are revoked. They are not, and
  `docs/licensing/GPL_CUTOFF.md` fixes the revision so that stays checkable.

Private development under a proprietary default may proceed in the meantime; external proprietary
distribution is gated on Questions 2, 3 and 4.
